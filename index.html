Client Background

Christopher Lupo will represent the owner of a used car dealership who wants to improve how vehicles are priced. One of the dealership's main challenges is determining fair and competitive prices because each vehicle has different characteristics that can affect its value. The client has hired two consultants to analyze historical used car data, identify the strongest predictors of vehicle prices, and develop machine learning models that could help the dealership make better pricing decisions.

Problem

Used car dealerships need to determine fair and competitive prices for vehicles. However, pricing can be challenging because factors such as mileage, model year, engine size, and fuel type can affect a vehicle's value. Understanding which factors are most closely related to price could help dealerships make better pricing decisions.

Research Question

Which vehicle characteristics are the strongest predictors of used car prices, and how accurately can machine learning models estimate vehicle prices?

Objective

Our goal is to identify the factors that have the strongest relationship with used car prices and develop machine learning models to predict vehicle prices. We will compare different models to determine which provides the most accurate predictions and how these results could help dealerships improve their pricing decisions.

Data exploration summary

Our dataset contains 4,009 used cars with an average price of approximately $44,553. We found that luxury brands such as Bugatti, Rolls-Royce, and Lamborghini had some of the highest average prices. Vehicle age also appeared to influence pricing, as older cars generally had lower average prices.

Mileage had a negative correlation of approximately -0.31 with price, suggesting that vehicles with higher mileage generally sell for less. We also compared fuel types and found that hybrid vehicles had an average price of approximately 51,426, compared to 44,539 for gasoline vehicles.

Finally, we examined engine size and found differences in average prices across engine sizes. Some unusually expensive luxury vehicles may have influenced these averages. Overall, our exploration suggests that vehicle age, mileage, brand, fuel type, and engine characteristics are worth investigating further when predicting used car prices.

We used LassoCV to test 30 different alpha values using five-fold cross-validation. This shows that optimizing alpha improved the model's ability to predict log used car prices, although the difference between training and testing scores suggests some overfitting.

5.2 Lasso Regression: coefficients

Step 4: Examine the Lasso coefficients (top 10 by absolute value)

The optimized Lasso Regression results show that mileage and vehicle age have the largest absolute coefficients, suggesting that both are important factors in predicting used car prices. 5.2 Lasso Regression: model comparison

Step 5: Compare Decision Tree and Lasso Regression

We compared the Decision Tree and optimized Lasso Regression models using their training and testing R² scores on log(price). The optimized Lasso Regression performed better, with a testing R² score of 81.69%, compared to 61.05% for the Decision Tree. Both models performed better on the training data than the testing data, suggesting some overfitting. Overall, the optimized Lasso Regression was the better-performing model for predicting log used car prices in our dataset.
